## ONE-PAGE SUMMARY

**Verdict for this section.** This is the most carefully instrumented one-lot system I have ever read, and it is trading a thesis it has never measured — and the instrument it would use to measure it is not yet pointed at the question the book asks. The machinery is a fade book on levels; on its own detector, collapsing the re-reads that inflate the row count, **first touches hold 85 of 147 resolved episodes = 57.8% [49.7, 65.5]**, and on the static kinds alone **42 of 80 = 52.5% [41.7, 63.1]** — a coin flip, on **33 distinct static price levels** observed over parts of three days (`r09_dedup.txt`). Targets are written by the model to clear a ratio gate — planned R:R mean **3.33**, median **2.70** (n=56 compliant) — against a tape that reaches the planned target **9 times in 56 (16.1% [8.7, 27.8])**. Losers exit at the stop **40 of 42** on the 65-row sensitivity — but **19 of 36** compliant losers had already run **≥ 0.5R our way** before dying there, which is a different fact and it matters. Mean realized R is **−0.031 [−0.444, +0.381]** (n=56). Nothing here is proven unprofitable; nothing here is proven at all, and the system's own Stage-A rule and its own pre-registered n=200-**episode** floor (`2026-09-02-detector-redesign.md:39-44`) already say so. The ideas that matter are therefore not new entries or new exits — they are (a) rebuilding the level measurement at the barriers a trade actually uses, and (b) the execution defects that would hurt on a live book before any edge could show.

**Three biggest problems (mine, this section).**

1. **The book put EIGHT one-lot stop orders on the wire for a one-contract system — and the arithmetic that did it is a law, not an accident.** [A] On 2026-09-04 from 10:25 to 10:38 CT the broker's own snapshots show a single scenario (NY S2 SHORT) accumulating **eight simultaneously `Accepted` sell-stop orders at 29590.50, one contract each** (`r05_snap.txt`, `r06_states.txt`: names 719f4bbb, c794d0ce, 4646df81, c5d4860a, 9fcd8c7c, 9b146ffa, 7dd07a19, 6a5da3fa, all `Accepted` in the 10:38:08 frame). The AddOn's `working_count` field reads **9** from 10:40 on because it counts the ninth order in transit while the fifteen-minute-old one is `CancelSubmitted`. Price topped at 29543.50; nothing filled; the 10:53:10 boot sweep cleared them. **The mechanism is arithmetic: placement was per-cycle and unconditional, retirement was per-order at fifteen minutes, and at a two-minute cadence that is a steady-state queue of 15 ÷ 2 ≈ 7.5 orders.** It reached 8 before the first reaper cancel even fired. This is `AUDIT-CHECKLIST.md` **class 79, "Silence read as death"** (`:1820`), and it was fixed by the reaper wave `ebe07d5f` — which IS the 10:53:10 boot I first credited only with a class-33 sweep. The running rev `36648655` carries the fix (`armed_executor.go:1085-1101`, the `reaperAlive` branch: "a quiet order that the broker still holds is left exactly as it is"). **What is still missing is a broker-keyed one-working-order-per-(plan, scenario, leg) leg, and a rule for the `reaperUnknown` branch, which today does nothing.** Details in SURPRISES §1. I did not act on it.
2. **The reward side is authored, not measured.** [T] Planned R:R on executed trades mean **3.33** / median **2.70** (n=56 compliant); realized payoff **1.83** (avg win $130.94 / avg loss $71.55, 17 W + 37 L); MFE ≥ planned target **9/56 = 16.1% [8.7, 27.8]**; MFE ≥ 2R 13/56 = 23.2% [14.1, 35.8]; MFE ≥ 1R 21/56 = 37.5% [26.0, 50.6]. Winners exit at the target 13/20; losers exit at the stop 40/42 (65-row sensitivity, the log's broker reasons). The model writes targets the tape does not visit. **What I can no longer claim is that the 2.0 floor is what makes it do so**: on the compliant set **10 of 56** trades cleared with a planned R:R *below* 2.0 (568, 569, 550, 587, 542, 523, 589, 562, 529, 582 — eight of them on the decision path), so the floor is not binding on the path that produced most of these trades. That is either a gate leak worth its own probe or a defect in my stop recovery; either way "the gate makes it worse" is a hypothesis this tape does not carry.
3. **The location premise is unmeasured where it is traded — and the detector that "measures" it answers a different question, on a corpus that shrinks by a third when the re-reads are collapsed — and tenfold on the kind the arms are pointed at.** [T] Three separate defects, each of which I got wrong in my first draft and each of which I verified myself:
   (i) **The barrier is not the trade's barrier.** All 677 `touch_outcomes` rows resolve on `horizon=12, k=3.0, exit_on='close'`, δ 4.24–4.85 → a **±12.7–14.5 point double barrier of 1-minute CLOSES inside 12 minutes** (`r03_touch.txt`). The compliant trades risk a **median 33.75 points** and live a **median 25.3 minutes**, with **41 of 58 longer than 13 minutes**. A 13-minute, 13-point statistic cannot point an arm whose stop is 2.5× the barrier and whose life is twice the horizon.
   (ii) **The rows are not episodes.** 677 rows collapse to **423 distinct (kind, price, open-time) episodes**; the static kinds sit on **33 distinct prices in total**. **RTH-L is 140 rows wearing 14 episodes at ONE price (29199.25)** — deduplicated it is **8 break / 4 hold / 2 ambiguous, p(break) 0.667 [0.391, 0.862], n=12**, not 82/124. My "66.1%" and "64/98 in NY" were row counts, and section 02 caught it before I did (`2026-09-05-vet-02-levels.md:53`).
   (iii) **The one kind with genuine n is the one that cannot be read this way.** VWAP is 199 rows and 199 episodes only because it is a *moving* line re-priced at each read — section 02's lookahead objection (their P9), not independent touches.
   `trade_excursions` has 0 rows (the writer IS wired — `auto_trader_clock.go:768`, class 56, in every binary since 09-03 10:28 — no trade has closed under it). The system cannot yet rank its own levels, the arm layer is pointed by doctrine, and **the pre-registered floor that already forbids every per-kind verdict here (n = 200 *episodes*, `detector-redesign.md:43`) is being violated by every kind in the table, mine included.**

**Three biggest opportunities.**

1. **Rebuild the level truth table at the trade's own barriers, on episodes not rows, and let it point the arms (the one-month project, §Q3).** The detector, the bars, the touch tables, a read-only per-cell CLI (`cmd/detector-report`), the n=200-episode floor and the expectancy endpoint all already exist. What is missing is not "a pre-registration" — it is (a) an episode key so a level is counted once per touch, (b) barriers set at the stop floor and a horizon at least the median hold, (c) the *joint* promotion question (favourable excursion before adverse), and (d) a kill date that reviews evidence rather than forcing a verdict. Evidence gate and kill criterion are restated in §Q3.
2. **Stop asking a reasoning model "wait?" every two minutes.** [T] Era: **8,854 executor cycles, 80.8 model-hours, 117 open intents (1.3% [1.1, 1.6]), 3 closes.** Post-0B (from 2026-09-02 07:49 CT): **1,428 cycles, 9.7 model-hours, 24 intents, 2 entries (589, 590 — both stopped), 0 closes, 19 refusals**, average 24.4 s per call. Under `plan_mode=strict` this loop cannot open; it spent 37 minutes on 09-03 (20:35–21:12) proposing the same refused short twelve times. Move the poll to a deterministic state machine and call the model on events; the plan card is where the model earns its keep.
3. **Derive targets and stops from the tape's own excursion distribution, then let the R:R floor be a filter — after ASIA is off, not beside it.** [T]/[I] MFE/risk median 0.67, p75 1.87; winners' MAE/risk median 0.37, p75 0.70, max 0.97. A 0.5R-exit counterfactual (n=58 sensitivity set) has mean R +0.021 [−0.157, +0.198] against the actual −0.078 [−0.480, +0.324] — an upper bound (1-minute extremes, no slip), not a result. **Sequence matters**: on the compliant set, removing ASIA takes planned R:R from mean 3.45 / max 18.73 (n=54) to **mean 2.86 / max 6.29 (n=38)**, lifts MFE ≥ target from 16.7% to **21.1%**, and takes the book from **−$452.40 to +$100.00**. A third of the "authored target" problem is an ASIA problem. Ship the session ruling first and re-measure, or you will build a target machine to fix a session.

## Q1 — What other bot traders I know do that this one doesn't

I have grounded every "this doesn't" in a line or a query, because this system has 79 audit classes and I found three things I was ready to call absent that are not (regime block, HTF veto, a digest chain into the planner). Each item carries [R]/[I], and what it would take here.

### 1.1 Entry timing

**What they do.** (a) Enter *with* the direction of the opening range rather than fading it — [R] Zarattini & Aziz 2023 (SSRN 4416622) tested exactly this on QQQ, 2016-01-01 → 2023-02-17: if the first 5-minute candle is bullish, **buy at market at the open of the second 5-minute candle**; stop at the opposite extreme of that first candle; target 10R else liquidate at the close; leverage obtained via TQQQ; $0.0005/share commission, **no spread and no slippage modelled**. It does **not** test a stop order through the range extreme, so the "stop-through OR-H" variant I propose below is **[I]**, mine, and inherits none of that paper's evidence. Note also that a 10R target would fail my own Q2.1 mind-changing test by construction. And [I] every desk I sat on ran some form of opening-range trade on NQ — my experience, untested here. (b) Trade the last thirty minutes with the day's direction — [R] Gao, Han, Li & Zhou 2018, *Market Intraday Momentum*, JFE 129(2) 394-414: on SPY 1993–2013 the first half-hour return (measured from the previous close) predicts the last half-hour, more strongly on high-volatility, high-volume, recession and macro-release days; (c) place the resting order **before** the level is reached and let queue position work — [I] a limit that is not on the book when price arrives is not a resting order.

**What this system does.** (a) OR-H/OR-L (first 5 minutes) and IB-H/IB-L are *seated levels* (`kernel/levels_intraday.go:111-139,171-183`), and the armable set is `fvg_entry, reject, breakdown_continue, breakup_continue, reclaim` (`kernel/armed.go:17-26`, dev; the boot line `stop-entry=on(reclaim)` confirms the running rev arms stop-entries for reclaim only). `breakout_retest` is shadowed by owner ruling after a negative replay (F4, 2026-08-28) — so the break of the range is traded, if at all, as a *retest limit*, never as a stop-through. On this tape the retest limit is the arm the marketable guard kills ("level accepted through — marketable, never placed", `trader/armed_executor.go:960`). [T] `breakout_retest` realized: n=9, 1 W / 8 L, −$581.50 (`/api/expectancy` by condition, ids 528, 534, 556, 557, 558, 562, 563, 589, 590) — all decision-path market entries citing a retest scenario, since the condition is never armed. That is the retest-at-market version losing; the stop-through version has not been run here.
(b) The session's window end and EOD flat are both 14:45 CT (`kernel/session_registry.go:107,109` — `WindowEndCT` = `FlatCT`; the flatten itself is `trader/auto_trader_clock.go:476` `enforceEODFlatAt`), with last-entry 14:30 (boot line). The last-half-hour window Gao et al. describe is 14:30–15:00 CT, so **only the back half is excluded by construction — the 14:30–14:45 leg is legal today** and has simply never been used: **0 of the 68 era entries fall in the 14:00, 15:00 or 16:00 CT hours** (`r04_more.txt`). ### 1.3 Volatility sizing

**What they do.** Hold dollar risk per trade constant by sizing off the stop distance (contracts = $risk ÷ stop pts ÷ point value); scale participation by ATR percentile. **[I] — desk practice, not a published result.** The nearest [R] is the portfolio-level volatility-scaling literature (Moreira & Muir 2017, *Volatility-Managed Portfolios*, JF 72(4)), which scales a **portfolio's** exposure by inverse realized variance at a monthly horizon to time the equity risk premium, and which is itself contested out of sample (Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan 2020, JFE). Neither speaks to per-trade sizing. [I] at one lot of MNQ the lever is imaginary — the minimum size *is* the size.

**What this system does.** Size is fixed at 1 by the Stage-A cap (`kernel/risk_limits.go:358-380`, `StageAContractCap`, env `STAGE_A_CONTRACT_CAP`), boot line `size=1`. ## Q3 — One codebase, one month: the project

**The project: rebuild the level truth table at the barriers a TRADE uses, on episodes rather than rows, and let the arms follow it.** Not a new entry, not a new exit. My first draft called this "pre-register the truth table". Three verifier lenses and my own re-measurement have changed what the project *is*, and the change is worth stating plainly rather than quietly editing the numbers.

**What I got wrong, and what it costs the project.**

1. **Pre-registration already exists.** `2026-09-02-detector-redesign.md:39-44` carries an **E3 edge rule** ("real tape shows an edge ONLY if its p(hold) Wilson interval lies strictly ABOVE the E2 stationary-bootstrap point estimate; overlap ⇒ no demonstrated edge") and a **ranking floor of n = 200 EPISODES** with 30 ≤ n < 200 reported descriptive-only. `expectancy/aggregate.go:503-512` carries `MinN = 30` for money cells. What has never existed is (a) an episode key, (b) barriers matched to the trade, (c) the coupling of a promoted cell to the arm gate, and (d) a kill date.
2. **The per-cell table already exists as a CLI.** `go run ./cmd/detector-report` prints p(hold) per kind / session / ordinal with n, a Wilson interval and the ambiguous share it excluded, read-only. The delta is persistence, the excursion columns, and the plan card — closer to a day than the two I quoted.

The 15-day depth is simply how long persistence has been running (#77, 08-26). The 1m table reaches 90 days on ~2026-11-17 by doing nothing. There is no knob to build.

So a promotion leg reading "median excursion ≥ 1.5×ATR5m" fails on two reads in three **for a measurement reason, not a market one** — my own kill criterion was pre-loaded to fire.

DEMAND: 139 rows, 18 episodes. Writing the episode key onto the outcome row **at detection time** is a prerequisite, not a nicety.

**Why this project and nothing else, still.** Every other idea in this report — targets from MFE, stops from MAE, session selection, break-vs-fade, the regime stamp — depends on knowing, per level kind × session × ordinal, what the tape does on the touch **at the barriers a trade actually risks**. The system has the detector, the bars, the read model and the seating machinery. What it does not have is an instrument aimed at its own question. A month is enough to build that; it is not enough to *prove an edge with it*, and I will not pretend otherwise.

**What it takes (code, small; owner ruling, one; data, the point).**
1. **The episode key.** Stamp a stable `(kind, price, first-open-ms)` episode id onto every `touch_outcomes` row at detection, and the matching `touch_episodes` id beside it. Without this every n in the table is a read count. **Half a day, and it is the whole project's foundation.**
2. **Re-cut the barriers.** Add a second resolution pass at the trade's own geometry: barrier = ±1× the live stop floor (1.5×ATR5m at the touch), horizon ≥ the median hold (25 min → 30 bars), `exit_on` ruled explicitly. Keep the existing ±3δ/12-bar pass beside it as the *detector-quality* diagnostic it is. **Two days, and it is what makes the table able to point an arm.**
3. **The truth table**: persist `cmd/detector-report`'s per-cell output nightly (kind × session × ordinal-1 — **the approach-speed bucket is DEFERRED until (1) lands**), with n **episodes**, hold, break, ambiguous, Wilson intervals, and the joint statistic below. Rendered on the plan card. **A day on top of the CLI.**
4. **The promotion rule, committed as a doc (this is the owner ruling).** My first draft's two-leg rule does not compose into money and I am replacing it. Leg 1 was P(direction); leg 2 was a **median** excursion, i.e. P(reach it | direction) = 0.50 **by construction** — so a cell that just cleared PROMOTED-BREAK reached a 1×floor target on ≈ 0.60 × 0.50 = 0.30 of touches while risking one floor: **≈ 0.30(+1R) − 0.70(−1R) = −0.40R, a losing cell by arithmetic.** PROMOTED-FADE was worse. The replacement is one number, and it is the number that pays:
   - a cell is **PROMOTED** when, over **n ≥ 200 resolved EPISODES spanning ≥ 20 session days** with ambiguous ≤ 25%, the **Wilson lower bound of P(favourable excursion ≥ 1× stop-floor BEFORE adverse excursion ≥ 1× stop-floor) exceeds 0.55** in the promoted direction, measured on the re-cut barriers of step 2;
   - P(direction) and the excursion quantiles are reported as **diagnostics, never as the gate**;
   - the E3 rule stands beside it: a cell whose interval overlaps its own stationary-bootstrap point estimate shows no demonstrated edge, whatever the Wilson-lo says;
   - a kind whose ambiguous share exceeds 35% at **n ≥ 30 episodes** is **UNMEASURABLE** and is removed from seating. My first draft set that bar at n ≥ 100 and then listed three kinds that cannot reach it: at n ≥ 100 **nothing on this tape is removed** (the only kinds with n ≥ 100 *rows* are VWAP 33.7%, RTH-L 11.4%, DEMAND 7.9%). At n ≥ 30 episodes the clause has teeth; pick one bar and let the examples obey it.
5. **The hypotheses, restated on the corrected corpus** so they cannot be fitted later — and note that **every one of them is below the pre-registered n=200-episode floor today**, which is the point:
   - **H1 — NY RTH-L is PROMOTED-BREAK.** Corrected prior: **8 break / 4 hold / 2 ambiguous, p(break) 0.667 [0.391, 0.862], n=12 episodes at ONE price (29199.25), 97 of the 98 NY rows from a single day (09-03), and none at all in the 09-04 NY session.** That is a hypothesis with almost nothing behind it, and I am registering it as such. It needs ~188 more episodes.

H2 is registered as a hypothesis **the current detector cannot decide**: if the ambiguity share has not fallen below 25% by the kill date, H2 resolves **UNMEASURABLE**, not FAILED. Its rows also carry section 02's lookahead objection.
   - **H3 — DEMAND zones are not promoted either way.** Deduplicated: 8 hold / 9 break / 1 ambiguous, n=17 episodes (18 prices' worth of rows collapse to 2 distinct prices).
6. **The arm gate leg**: arms are placed only at PROMOTED cells, in the promoted direction; everything else is authored, graded, shadowed (the 0C machinery already does "authored + E8-scored, never placed"). One leg in `EntryGate`, both seams (the project's own one-gate law). **A day.**
7. **The kill criterion — a review, not a verdict.** The date is **2026-10-16**, which is **29 weekdays from 2026-09-08** (30 only if the 09-07 Labor Day session is counted; the registry's `half_days` map lists 09-06, 11-25, 11-26 and 12-23 — there is no 09-07 entry, so the running system will not shorten it). On that date: if **no cell is promoted in either direction**, the fade book goes **shadow-only** — arms off, authoring and grading continue — until a cell promotes. If H1 promotes and no fade cell does, the arm layer flips to stop-entries at RTH-L and the fade book is retired. If the ambiguous share of a *promoted* cell rises above 25% for five consecutive days, the promotion is revoked. **What the date does NOT do is convert "not enough evidence" into "no edge"** — section 09 is right about that (`2026-09-05-vet-09-top-ten.md:130`), and a review that can only return PROMOTED or RETIRED is a review that will lie to you. The third verdict is **INCONCLUSIVE — remain shadow**, and on today's episode counts it is the likeliest one.

**The evidence gate for the project itself** (so the month cannot be declared a success by narrative): the deliverable is the table keyed on **episodes**, with the barrier definition printed in its own header, ≥ 20 session days per cell, and a signed promotion sheet.
