### 2.3 My answer: what the LLM authors, what is mechanical

**The LLM authors:** (1) the read — regime, day type, the bias-tree branch it took, in prose; (2) a level shortlist with a role and a reason for each (react / target / invalidation), drawn from the machine's seated pool, with any addition flagged as the model's own; (3) **one primary scenario** (condition, direction, the level, the invalidation line in prose) and at most one alternate on the other side for a `neutral` day; (4) the no-trade reasons it sees that the calendar does not. That is reading a situation, which is what the model is for.

**Mechanical, from the tape, with no model input:** (1) entry = the level (already the arm's entry); (2) stop = `composeArmStop` as it stands (anchor beyond nearest seated level + 2 ticks, floored at 1.5×ATR5m) — this exists, and it is the right *ownership* of the stop; whether "2 ticks beyond the nearest level" is the right *placement* is **[I]** and has a published argument against it (see the Osler note at the end of this section); (3) **target = a rule from the store's own MAE/MFE distribution**, e.g. the level chain's next seated level capped at the empirical p75 MFE (**1.85R** on the compliant 54) — not a number the model chose. **Say what that rule can and cannot claim:** on this store *no* fixed-R target has positive expectancy (T = 0.5R → +0.035R per trade, 0.67R → −0.069R, 1.0R → −0.099R, 1.79R → −0.039R, 2.0R → −0.062R, 3.0R → −0.062R, against realized −0.050R, n=54), and MFE here is censored by the brackets a new rule would replace. The distribution is saying the *entries* carry no edge at any target, not that the target is misplaced. This item fixes **who owns the number**; it is not an expectancy claim.
