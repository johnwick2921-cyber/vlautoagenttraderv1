## One-page summary

**Verdict.** I would keep a persistent, provenance-bearing structural map, give the planner a compact active-priority view, and authorize trades through a separate setup/risk decision. The present level layer partly conflates these jobs. It can remove a still-meaningful reference because it loses ranking, proximity, grade or capacity competition. That is not evidence that its market structure has become invalid. Conversely, retaining a line does not establish a reason to fade or break it. The exact score ladder is unvalidated; neither it nor a nearest-N replacement has demonstrated superior net opportunity outcomes here. This is a recommendation for an auditable comparison, not a request to implement a change.

**Three biggest problems.**

1. **Selection is narrower than the name “full pool” suggests.** It already applies proximity, confluence eligibility, collapse, seating and a 2×cap truncation. **Three biggest opportunities.**

1. **Preserve structure through changes in priority [I].** Keep origin session, timeframe, zone edges, confirmation time and state-transition history; reduce active attention when far away without erasing the reference. Let a broken level remain available as a possible target or opposite-side retest, with a new trade hypothesis required. ## 1. Inventory: definitions, windows, grading, seating, touches and ambiguity

**What the table measures.** It inventories all 41 enumerated kinds, including those historically seated but absent from this one-day candidate census. Pool frequency is seated candidate rows divided by recorded rows of that kind, **not** all detector emissions and **not** plan-session frequency. The census is September 4, 00:30:54.769–12:01:01.727 CT, candidate IDs 1–360. Missing rows do not mean the detector found nothing. The source window can suppress a kind before this census, and the pool itself is truncated. The older 47-plan-session label census is retained as historical data, not substituted for candidate-pool frequency.

For useful forward observations I freeze each kind/price at its **first recorded read**, including cut candidates, and look for a range touch in the next 60 complete one-minute bars. There are 84 first-exposure kind/price keys, 82 with a complete horizon. From the touch, the next 12 bars' closes resolve beyond level±3Δ as hold/break relative to the immediately preceding close. Δ=5.096354544 points is estimated from the five preceding complete CME days; it is frozen before the exposure. “A” is unresolved after the full 12 bars; “censored” means the follow-up ended earlier. Touching a level is not a fill, and a hold is not a winning trade. This explicitly differs from D1′'s new-touch predicate, which additionally requires the previous bar not to bracket the level (`kernel/detector_d1prime.go:129`). It is a **first-post-read reach/reaction diagnostic**, not a parity replay.

The first bar containing the read instant is discarded; scan starts at the next full minute. As-of price uses the last fully completed bar. No future “ever seated” label is used. A recorded read proves a price was known then; it does not recover the actual original formation/confirmation time. Dynamic VWAP/ON/OR references are frozen-price opportunities, not the subsequent moving line. Zone tests use recorded midpoint because zone edges are missing. Same-bar stop/target sequencing, spread, queue and execution are unmeasurable. Ordinary Wilson intervals below are descriptive binomial intervals; repeated prices and one common session-day prevent treating them as independent strategy-confidence intervals. **No kind meets an independent n≥30 outcome standard in this new forward inventory.**

## 2. Which kinds I would retain, what the tape supports, and what I trust

**My map judgment [I].** I would retain separately named prior CME-session high/low/close, the chosen RTH-window high/low, verified settlement when available, completed overnight extremes, developing/final OR and IB, session VWAP and its envelope, prior value structure, weekly/monthly extremes and confirmed 5m/15m/1h/4h structure. These let a planner state where the auction is, what would invalidate its interpretation and what lies beyond the first target. This is an information-design judgment, not a claim that each price contains institutional orders. An unfilled 4h demand base does not lose its origin merely because nearer intraday lines win twelve seats. A break changes its hypothesis; it does not erase the historical auction.

I would retain EQH/EQL as observed pivot clusters, zones as **bands** with provenance, and round-number alignment as an annotation. I would not allocate mandatory additional active seats to every round number or every overlapping zone. Nor would I remove them from measurement because their ranking is low. An eVWAP anchor, a ±2σ boundary and a central VWAP describe different parts of the distribution; grouping them in a compact family display is plausible, but restricting all three to the nearest single tradable reference is an untested policy change.

**What this tape supports [T].** RTH-H seats 13/15 times, while EQH seats 0/4 and OB 8/117 in the retained candidate pool (all denominators and Wilson intervals in Question 1). Those are facts about selection, not votes cast by the market. The post-read first-exposure diagnostic reaches **17/47 initially seated levels =36.2% [24.0%,50.5%]**, versus **5/35 initially cut=14.3% [6.3%,29.4%]** over a fixed hour. Resolved holds are **5/11=45.5% [21.3%,72.0%]** and **1/4=25.0% [4.6%,69.9%]** respectively (`E:21`–`E:22`). Distance, kind, time of read and selection policy differ; no inference about superiority follows. The matched check in Question 3 controls only some of those differences. The 58-trade losing realized population neither validates nor falsifies any single level kind.

**When judgment and tape disagree.** I trust accurately defined structure for describing context, and I require properly timed, executable outcomes before declaring an edge or a trading ban. “No statistically demonstrated advantage” is not “no informational value,” and neither licenses deleting a prior low or automatically fading it. The old replay's subgroups, null comparison and EQH/eVWAP highlights are exploratory historical calculations, not an independent promotion gate.

**External support has a narrow boundary [R].** Carol Osler's 2000 *Support for Resistance* tests published levels from six FX firms against intraday exchange-rate trend interruptions; the effect varies across firms and currencies. It supports investigating identified levels, not this system's weights or an MNQ fade rule. [Primary NY Fed paper](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf).

Osler's bank-order study, published in 2003, examines almost 9,700 stop-loss/take-profit orders from September 1999–April 2000, focusing on dollar-yen, dollar-pound and euro-dollar. It links different round-number clustering patterns to reversal and continuation mechanisms. This is FX customer-order evidence, not centralized NQ/MNQ order-book evidence. My inference is to measure approach, crossing and retest separately. A coarse MNQ hold-rate null using a different band is **not a replication or refutation** of that study. [Primary working paper](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf), [published-version identification](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr125.html).
